**Behaviour Under Addiction: Dynamic Choice Between Smoking and Vaping (Job Marker Paper)**

E-cigarettes are addictive but less harmful than combustible cigarettes. Taxes on e-cigarettes may discourage vaping, but could increase more harmful cigarette demand through substitution. I study this policy trade-off using a dynamic discrete--continuous model of joint demand. Households choose which products to buy and how much to purchase. Two product-specific addiction stocks link past purchases to current choices and evolve with the quantities consumed. The model is estimated using NielsenIQ household panel data from 2019 to 2024 by maximum likelihood. A surrogate neural network approximates continuation values across states and parameter values, making estimation feasible. Addiction plays a central role in determining how households respond to price changes, differently on participation and on quantity choices. Cigarettes and e-cigarettes are substitutes: a permanent 1% increase in the e-cigarette price raises cigarette quantity by 1.4%, and a corresponding increase in the cigarette price raises e-cigarette quantity by 1.8%. Own-price responses weaken with addiction. An e-cigarette ban sends a third of vapers to smoking, whereas a cigarette ban sends almost all smokers to abstinence.

The impact of discount retail stores on household consumption and wellbeing: evidence from the UK

This paper examines how new discounter openings affect households’ shopping behaviour, grocery spending, diet quality and well-being. I combine household purchase data and survey data with mental health measures in the UK. To address endogenous store location, I estimate a discrete-choice model of chain entry and use predicted opening probabilities in a doubly robust staggered-adoption event study. The first discounter opening in a postcode district increases households’ discounter spending share by 5.3 percentage points, with roughly 70% diverted from the four largest supermarkets, but leaves shopping frequency unchanged. Households spend 1.0% less on equivalent quantities, driven by switching stores rather than lower prices for their existing basket. Despite these savings, nutritional quality remains unchanged across key dietary measures. Similarly, openings within walking distance have no detectable effects on financial satisfaction, psychological distress, health or life satisfaction. *Discounter entry changes where households shop and reduces grocery costs, but these savings do not translate into measurable improvements in diet or well-being*.
